1Your Two Jobs

You sell on two fronts. Same company, two completely different pitches. Don't mix them up.

🍌 Job A — Land banana customers

Who: family-owned banana ripeners/importers (the ~39-name pipeline).

What you're selling: reliable, owner-accountable drayage of their Dole/Chiquita containers out of Port of Wilmington.

Cycle: slow, relationship-driven, high lifetime value (a dedicated lane = recurring revenue for years).

Style: voice-first, respect-first, become a known face in the produce world. Depth over volume.

🚛 Job B — Sell backhaul capacity

Who: direct shippers along I-95 (Boston → Wilmington).

What you're selling: our 40 empty southbound containers/week at a discount — pure-profit for us.

Cycle: faster, more transactional, lower value per deal but higher volume of at-bats.

Style: high-volume cold calling/emailing. Great training ground — start here.

Ramp order: cut your teeth on Job B (fast reps, low stakes, quick commission) while you study the banana dossiers. Earn the Job A Tier-1 accounts once you've proven you won't fumble them.

2The Pitch & Talk Tracks

The first 8 seconds are the whole game. Their only job: earn the next 30 seconds of airtime — not to pitch.

Cold-call opener — Style A (permission-based, use this first)

Backhaul cold call · opener "Hey, [Name]? … [Name], it's [you] with BASE Carriers in Wilmington. Look — I know I'm calling out of the blue. Can I take 30 seconds and tell you why, and you can tell me to bug off if it's not relevant?"

Why it works: naming it as a cold call wins 2.1× vs. pretending; giving them an out makes them say "go ahead" 60%+ of the time. Then:

The pitch (after "go ahead") "We run 40 containers a week up I-95 between Wilmington and Boston. Our trucks roll back south empty 3–4 days a week regardless. I'd rather give one shipper in [City] a standing discounted slot than run empty — are you shipping anything south out of [City]?"

Voicemail + text (always pair them — 4× the callback)

22-sec voicemail "Hey [Name], [you] at BASE Carriers in Wilmington — calling because we run empty containers south on I-95 every week, thought we might save [Company] money on outbound to the Carolinas. I'll text you the rate sheet. Number's [cell], again [cell]. Thanks."
Text within 5 minutes "[Name], just left you a vm — [you] from BASE Carriers. Question on your outbound from [City] heading south. Easier by text? STOP to opt out."

Banana-customer opener (Job A — different game)

These are owners of family businesses. Lead with the local/relationship proof, not capacity.

Banana customer · warm/cold call "Hi [Name] — [you] with BASE Carriers out of the Port of Wilmington. We pull Dole and Chiquita containers off the port every day — we actually already run for [neighbor/known name]. I'm the kind of outfit where you get the owner on the phone, not a call center. Figured I'd reach out directly — who handles your drayage off the port right now?"

The closing ask (the puppy-dog close)

Close — low-commitment trial "Tell you what — give me 2 lanes you ran last week with destinations and rough rates. I'll quote them within 24 hours. If I beat your number, would you run one load with us as a test?"
Voice mechanics beat the script: stand up, smile while dialing, speak ~140 wpm (slower than feels natural), drop your pitch ~10%, and after you ask a question — shut up. The silence makes them talk. Count to 5 if you have to.

3Discovery — 3 Questions, In Order

After the opener earns you 30 seconds, don't pitch — ask. Listen more than you talk.
#QuestionWhat it tells you
1"Who handles your outbound freight today — in-house, broker, or asset carrier?"Broker-friendly = good fit. "Own trucks" = harder. "A couple brokers" = wide open.
2"What's a typical week look like out of [City] going south — loads and destinations?"Is there freight to fight for at all? "Mostly inbound here" = polite exit.
3"Roughly what are you paying per load on those lanes today?"~60% deflect — ask anyway. A number = a benchmark you can beat by 30–40%.
Buying signals (lean in): they give specific lanes, volunteer rate complaints, mention carrier problems, or ask about insurance/onboarding/payment.
Weak signals (exit politely): "we're all set," one-word answers, "just send me an email" with no engagement.

4Objection Handling — The 6 You'll Hear Daily

An objection is information, not rejection. Most are reflexes — answer calmly and keep going.
"We already have a carrier / we're all set."
"Totally expected — I'm not asking you to fire anyone. I'm asking to be the truck that's already going your direction when your guy is tight. Give me one lane to quote; if I don't beat it, you've lost nothing."
"We run our own trucks."
"Smart — then you know the pain of empty miles. We're not trying to replace your fleet, just fill the southbound lane your trucks don't run. When your own equipment is committed, we're the overflow."
"You're just another broker."
"Fair — and most brokers will post your load to whoever's cheapest on DAT. We're not that. We run a fixed Wilmington↔Boston corridor, our own chassis relationships, TWIC drivers, 90%+ repeat lanes. You're getting a scheduled truck, not a load board."
"Just send me an email." (the brush-off)
"Happy to — what's the best address? And so it's not just another email in the pile: is there one southbound lane I should quote in it? … Great, I'll have it to you today and follow up Thursday." (Pin a specific next step or it dies.)
"Your rate's too high."
"What number are you working against? … I'd rather know than guess. Our truck's already making that trip, so I've got room a van carrier doesn't — let me sharpen the pencil on that exact lane." (Never drop below the floor without Amedeo.)
"We've been burned by brokers before."
"I get it — that's exactly why you'll have my cell, not a 1-800 number. I dispatch, I bill, I make the rate call. If something goes sideways at 2 AM, you're talking to the owner, not a claims department."
The owner-accountability card is unbeatable. No large 3PL and almost no other small broker can say "you'll talk to me, the owner — yes or no in five minutes, not next week." Play it often.

7Cutting Through the Broker Noise

Mid-market shippers get 40–70 broker emails a week and 78% never engage. You must NOT sound like a broker.

Sound like an asset carrier, not a broker

BASE's reality (40 boxes/week, fixed corridor, owned chassis relationships, TWIC drivers, 90%+ repeat) is structurally a small dedicated carrier. Talk like one:

✅ Asset-carrier voice

  • Volume in units: "40 containers/week," "173 last month"
  • Named geography: "Conley Terminal Boston," "Port of Wilmington"
  • Schedule, not availability: "Tuesdays & Thursdays"
  • "Our trucks roll regardless"
  • "You'll talk to me — the owner"

❌ Broker noise (sounds like everyone)

  • "We have capacity available in your lanes"
  • "50,000+ vetted carrier network"
  • "Competitive rates, 24/7 tracking"
  • "Just following up / bumping this up"
Banned subject-line words (auto-archived by shippers): capacity · lanes · partner · backhaul · broker · freight · follow-up.
Use instead: scheduled · southbound · empty · [City]-to-[City] · slot · every Tuesday.
Don't say "backhaul" to a shipper — it signals "one-time, flaky." Say "scheduled southbound capacity," a "standing slot," or a "continuous-move discount."

8Prospect FAQ — What They Ask Back

Know these cold. Fumbling a basic question kills credibility.
They ask…You say…
"Are you insured?"Yes — auto liability + cargo above broker minimums; BMC-84 bonded. I'll send the COI.
"What if a load is late or damaged?"You call me — the owner — directly. No claims department, no runaround. We make it right.
"What are your payment terms?"Standard net terms; we can talk a flat weekly invoice with no surprise accessorials on a standing lane.
"Can you scale if we grow?"We run 40+ boxes/week on a fixed corridor and are adding carriers every quarter — yes, with notice.
"Do you have references?"We're the drayage partner for family banana houses across the Northeast — happy to connect you once we've run a load or two.
"What's your equipment?"40' intermodal containers on chassis, power-only, TWIC-cleared drivers out of Wilmington.

The multichannel sequence (3× better than calls alone)

Your name should hit each prospect ~8 times in 14 days across email, call, and text. Most brokers quit at touch 2 — 60% of replies come from touches 3–7.

12Guardrails & Compliance — Non-Negotiable

Commission reps optimize for their own check. These rules protect the company and you.
RuleWhy
Never quote below the floor without Amedeo. Everett floor = $1,400.You sell on margin, not revenue. Buying business with our margin loses money.
We only take freight from DIRECT shippers, never other brokers.BASE is a licensed broker — taking broker freight is double-brokering. Hard no.
Don't promise capacity ops can't cover.A blown delivery on a new account burns the relationship + our Dole/Chiquita reputation.
Amedeo approves new-customer credit before you commit.Getting stuck with a non-paying customer is worse than no customer.
Log every touch in the CRM.If it's not logged, it didn't happen — and you can't improve what you don't track.

Outreach law (don't create legal exposure)

  • Real "from" name + BASE physical address in the footer

  • No deceptive subject lines

📱 Text — TCPA

  • B2B cold text is governed by TCPA — include "STOP to opt out"
  • Honor opt-outs immediately
  • Business hours; use the BASE A2P-registered number
