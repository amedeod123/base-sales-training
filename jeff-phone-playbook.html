1 The First 8 Seconds

The opener's only job is to earn the next 30 seconds — not to pitch. Naming that it's a cold call beats pretending by 2×. Give them an out and 60%+ say "go ahead."

Cold opener — use this first"Hey, [Name]? … [Name], it's Jeff with BASE Carriers down in Wilmington. Look, I know I'm calling out of the blue — can I take 30 seconds to tell you why, and you can tell me to buzz off if it's not relevant?"
The pitch — after they say "go ahead" (shipper / backhaul)"We run a steady string of trucks up I-95 between Wilmington and Boston every week, and they roll back south empty most days. I'd rather give one shipper in [City] a discounted standing slot than run empty — are you shipping anything south out of [City]?"
The pitch — produce / banana house (different game)"Hi [Name] — Jeff with BASE Carriers out of the Port of Wilmington. We pull Dole and Chiquita containers off the port every day, and we already run for family houses around the Northeast. I'm the kind of outfit where you get a real person on the phone, not a call center. Who's handling your drayage off the port right now?"
🎙️ Voice beats script: stand up, smile while it rings, talk ~140 wpm (slower than feels right), drop your pitch ~10%. After you ask a question — shut up. Count to 5. The silence makes them talk.

No answer? Voicemail + text, always paired (4× the callback)

22-second voicemail"Hey [Name], Jeff at BASE Carriers in Wilmington — calling because we run trucks south on I-95 every week and thought we might save [Company] money on outbound. I'll shoot you a text. My cell's [###], again [###]. Thanks."
Text within 5 minutes"[Name], just left you a VM — Jeff w/ BASE Carriers. Quick question on your outbound out of [City] heading south. Easier by text? Reply STOP to opt out."

2 Discovery — 3 Questions, In Order

Once the opener buys you 30 seconds, don't pitch — ask. Listen more than you talk.

#AskWhat it tells you
1"Who handles your outbound today — in-house, a broker, or an asset carrier?""A couple brokers" = wide open. "Own trucks" = overflow play only.
2"What's a typical week look like out of [City] going south — loads and where to?"Is there freight to fight for at all? "Mostly inbound" = polite exit.
3"Roughly what are you paying per load on those lanes now?"~60% dodge — ask anyway. A number is a benchmark you can work.

Lean in when they give specific lanes, gripe about rates/carriers, or ask about insurance/payment. Exit politely on "we're all set," one-word answers, or "just email me" with zero engagement.

3 The 6 Objections You'll Hear Daily

An objection is information, not rejection. Most are reflexes — stay calm and keep rolling.

"We already have a carrier / we're all set."
"Totally expected — I'm not asking you to fire anyone. I want to be the truck already going your way when your guy's tight. Give me one lane to quote; if I don't beat it, you've lost nothing."
"We run our own trucks."
"Smart — then you know the pain of empty miles. We're not replacing your fleet, just filling the lane your trucks don't run. When your equipment's committed, we're the overflow."
"You're just another broker."
"Fair — most brokers post your load to whoever's cheapest. We're not that. Fixed Wilmington–Boston corridor, our own chassis relationships, TWIC drivers, mostly repeat lanes. You're getting a scheduled truck, not a load board."
"Just send me an email." (the brush-off)
"Happy to — what's the best address? And so it's not just another email in the pile: is there one southbound lane I should quote in it? … Great, I'll have it to you today." (Pin a specific next step or it dies.)
"Your rate's too high."
"What number are you working against? I'd rather know than guess. Let me sharpen the pencil on that exact lane." → Never drop the price yourself. Bring it to Amedeo.
"We've been burned by brokers before."
"I get it — that's exactly why you get my cell, not a 1-800 number. If something goes sideways at 2 AM, you're talking to a real person here, not a claims department."

💪 Your unbeatable card: "You'll talk to a real person who can give you a yes in five minutes, not next week." No giant 3PL can say that. Play it often.

4 The Close — Low-Commitment Trial

The puppy-dog close"Tell you what — give me 2 lanes you ran last week with destinations and rough rates. I'll get you a quote back fast. If I beat your number, would you run one load with us as a test?"

You're not closing a contract — you're closing the next step. "Send me 2 lanes" is a win. Log it and feed it to Amedeo for pricing.

⛔ DON'T SAY THIS

You asked for this list specifically. Burn it into your head before you dial.

  • Never quote a rate or floor without Amedeo. If they push for a number, get their number and say "let me sharpen the pencil and get right back to you." Pricing runs through Amedeo, period.
  • Never reveal margin, the carrier rate, or what we pay drivers. The customer only ever hears their price. What's behind it is nobody's business.
  • Don't say the word "backhaul" to a shipper — it screams "one-time, flaky." Say scheduled southbound standing slot [City]-to-[City].
  • Banned broker-noise words (they get you auto-deleted): capacity · lanes · partner · backhaul · broker · freight · just following up. Talk in units and schedule instead: "trucks every Tuesday," "boxes a week."
  • Don't promise capacity or a delivery window ops hasn't confirmed. A blown delivery on a new account torches the relationship — and our Dole/Chiquita name. "Let me confirm the truck and call you right back" is always a fine answer.
  • Don't invent payment terms. "Standard net terms" is your line. Anything custom = Amedeo.
  • Don't name-drop specific customers as references without permission. Say "family banana houses across the Northeast," not real account names.
  • Don't trash competitors by name. Sell what we do; don't smear what they do.
  • Don't oversell the insurance details. "Auto liability + cargo, BMC-84 bonded, I'll send the COI" — that's it. Don't freelance coverage numbers.
