Posting Loads on the DAT Load Board

How to cover a load with a carrier — and price it right.

← Back to training home

1What DAT is & when you'll use it

DAT is the biggest freight load board. Brokers post loads; carriers search for them.

You'll use DAT in three situations:

SituationWhat you do on DAT
You booked freight and need a truckPost the load so carriers find it and call/email you with rates
You're quoting a customer and need to price itCheck DAT Rate View (market rates) so you don't underbid or overpay
You have an empty truck to fill (backhaul)Post truck availability or search loads for that lane
The point of posting a load: get the load in front of hundreds of carriers at once instead of cold-calling them one by one. A well-posted load gets covered fast and at a fair rate.

2How to post a load — step by step

Origin & destination. City/state for pickup and delivery. Be exact — the city drives who sees it.
Equipment type. Pick the right one or the wrong carriers see it: Power Only (carrier brings the tractor only — our port containers), Reefer (refrigerated — bananas/produce), Van (dry boxed freight), Flatbed (lumber, steel, building materials).
Full or Partial. Almost always Full (FTL) for us.
Weight & length. e.g. 40,000 lbs. Don't overstate weight (scares carriers off).
Pickup & delivery dates. The available date(s). Carriers filter by date hard — get it right.
Rate. Post your target rate, or leave blank to collect bids. Anchor high if you post a number — you negotiate down, never up.
Contact info. Your name, phone (carriers call/text first), and email. Make it easy to reach you.
Reference #. Your internal load number so you can match the call to the load.
Comment box. The most important free-text field — see §3.

3The comment box — where you win or lose carriers

It's character-limited (~140). The structured fields cover origin/dest/equipment/rate, so use the comment for what filters and sells the load.

Lead with the deal-breakers (so unqualified carriers self-select out) and the perks (so the right ones lean in):

DEDICATED 40/wk. Power-only, port supplies chassis. TWIC REQUIRED. Drop & hook by 5AM, return empty to Wilmington port. Quick pay.
Put in the comment…Why
TWIC REQUIRED / credentials#1 filter — saves calls from carriers who can't run it
Power-only / chassis suppliedTells them they don't bring a trailer + removes the chassis worry
Drop & hook or live unloadSets the expectation up front
DEDICATED / weekly volumeThe hook — carriers chase recurring lanes, not one-offs
Quick pay + your numberDrives fast response (text beats call for owner-ops)
Don't: dump origin/dest/rate into the comment (they're already structured fields) — you'll waste your 140 characters. And don't advertise a higher weight than the load actually is.

4Price it with DAT Rate View first

Never post a rate blind. Check the market before you quote a customer or pay a carrier.

DAT Rate View shows the recent average spot and contract rate for a lane. Use it to:

  • Quote your customer with confidence (don't underbid your own margin away).
  • Know what carriers expect so you don't overpay — or post a rate so low nobody calls.
  • Spot a good lane — if the market rate is well above what you can cover it for, that's margin.
Rule: you make money on the spread (customer rate − carrier rate). Rate View protects both sides of that spread. Always confirm BASE's floor/target with Amedeo before committing.

5Posting truck availability (backhaul)

The flip side: when we have an empty truck, post the truck to find a load for it.

For the empty southbound leg, post available capacity: origin (e.g. Boston area), destination region (I-95 south), equipment (40' container / van), available date. Shippers and their brokers search trucks the same way carriers search loads.

Broker rule — don't forget it: BASE can only take freight from direct shippers, never from another broker. When a "load" you find on the board is posted by a broker, that's double-brokering — a hard no. Use the board to find shippers, not to resell another broker's freight.

6Handling responses & vetting the carrier

✅ Do

  • Answer fast — first broker to respond often wins the truck
  • Repost to stay near the top of search results
  • Confirm MC/DOT, authority, insurance & TWIC before you commit (ops/the system checks this)
  • Get the rate con signed before the truck rolls

🚫 Don't

  • Post a load you can't actually cover
  • Book a carrier you haven't vetted
  • Quote below BASE's floor without Amedeo
  • Accept a load from another broker

7What carriers will ask you

When a carrier calls or texts on your posted load, they're qualifying it fast. Have these answers ready before you post — fumbling them loses the truck.
They'll ask…Have this ready
"Where's it pick up and deliver?"Exact pickup & delivery city + facility name + address
"When does it pick up / deliver?"Pickup date + window; delivery date + appointment time; whether it's a hard appointment or first-come-first-served
"What is it / how heavy?"Commodity + weight (e.g. 40,000 lbs produce). Will it scale legal?
"What equipment?"Van / Reefer / Flatbed / Power Only. If reefer: the temp setpoint and continuous vs. cycle-sentry
"Live or drop?"Live load / live unload, or drop-and-hook, at each end
"What's it pay?"Your rate, all-in (fuel included). Know your number and your ceiling
"Any special requirements?"TWIC, port/terminal code, chassis (supplied or theirs), gate fee, lumper, tarps, food-grade washout, hazmat, overweight/tri-axle, scale ticket
"Who pays the gate/port fee?"State it up front (e.g. carrier covers the port gate fee)
"Detention / TONU?"Your policy. Never promise a TONU or detention without Amedeo's OK.
"How and when do you pay? Quick pay?"Pay terms (e.g. net 30) and whether quick pay (1–3 day) is available and at what fee
"Do I have to track? What app?"Yes — GPS tracking is required (MacroPoint / tracking link). A fee applies if they don't track
"Is the appointment already set?"Whether the appt is made or the carrier schedules it
Tip: answer crisply and confidently. A carrier who senses you don't know the load details assumes it's a problem load and moves on.

8What to get FROM the carrier before you book

You never dispatch a truck you haven't vetted. Collect and verify all of this first — it protects the load, the customer, and BASE.

The biggest broker mistake: dispatching a truck before confirming active authority and valid insurance. If that truck damages or steals the load, BASE is on the hook. Vet first, every time.

9The full process — post to paid

Every covered load runs this pipeline. Don't skip a step.
Post / source the load on DAT (or call your network).
Qualify the carrier & negotiate — answer their questions, get their all-in rate, agree a number.
Vet the carrier — FMCSA authority, safety, COI (auto + cargo, BASE as cert holder), not on DNU.
Set them up — if new: broker-carrier agreement, W-9, COI on file, factoring NOA.
Send the Rate Confirmation (rate con) — all load details + the rate. Carrier signs and returns it before the truck moves.
Enter the load in the TMS (Masport / Rose Rocket) and assign carrier + driver.
Send dispatch instructions — full stop details, appointment/booking #s, references, requirements, your contact.
Set up tracking — MacroPoint / tracking link; confirm the driver is sharing location.
Monitor pickup → transit → delivery — confirm pickup, ETAs, (reefer temps), pre-delivery check.
Collect the POD (and empty-return receipt on port loads) and note any OS&D / rejection.
Apply fees + invoice the customer; carrier submits their invoice + POD.
Pay the carrier (or factor) per terms / quick pay, and close the load.

10Start to finish — a full worked example

One container load, every step, nothing skipped. Example: a reefer container of produce, Port of Wilmington DE → Everett MA, power-only, TWIC, ~40,000 lbs. Carrier in this example: "ABC Transport LLC."

① Post / source the load

You post on DAT: Power Only, Wilmington DE → Everett MA, available date, 40,000 lbs, your contact, and the comment: "Power-only, port supplies chassis. TWIC REQUIRED. Reefer container — drop & hook by 5 AM, return empty to Wilmington port. Quick pay."

② Carrier responds — qualify & negotiate

ABC Transport: "Got a TWIC driver in Wilmington. What's it pay all-in?" You: "Picks up at the Port of Wilmington tomorrow 8 AM–4 PM, reefer container of produce, 40k. Delivers Everett MA the next morning by 5 AM, drop & hook, then you return the empty to the port. Port supplies the chassis, you cover the $32 gate fee, TWIC required, GPS tracking required. I've got [your number] on it." ABC: "Can do [their number]."

You agree the all-in rate in writing (text/email). Nothing moves until it's confirmed in writing.

③ Vet the carrier

Before you commit, you verify:

  • FMCSA: ABC's MC/DOT authority is active, not revoked or pending cancellation; safety rating not Unsatisfactory.
  • Insurance: their COI is on file and unexpired — auto liability ≥ $1M, cargo covering the load, with BASE as certificate holder.
  • Not on the Do-Not-Use list.

If ABC is brand new to BASE, you onboard them first: send the broker-carrier agreement + W-9, get the signed COI, and capture any factoring NOA.

④ Collect carrier & trip info

You get: legal name (ABC Transport LLC), MC#, DOT#, driver name + cell, tractor #, confirmation the driver holds a TWIC, factoring company + NOA (if they factor), and agreed pay terms (e.g., quick-pay rate).

⑤ Send the Rate Confirmation (rate con)

RATE CONFIRMATION — Load BASE-XXXX Carrier: ABC Transport LLC (MC###### / DOT######) Equipment: Power Only (port supplies reefer container + chassis) Commodity: Produce, ~40,000 lbs · Reefer container, setpoint per BOL PICKUP: Port of Wilmington, DE — [date] 0800–1600 · TWIC required · $32 gate fee (carrier) DELIVERY: [Receiver], Everett, MA — [date] by 0500 · drop & hook RETURN: empty container back to Port of Wilmington, DE RATE: $[all-in] · Quick pay available REQUIRED: GPS tracking the entire trip; signed POD; empty-return receipt Sign & return before dispatch.

ABC signs and returns it. Now it's a binding agreement.

⑥ Enter the load & assign the carrier in the TMS

You enter / confirm the load in Masport / Rose Rocket and assign ABC Transport + the driver, so the whole team can see it's covered and who owns it.

⑦ Dispatch the driver

DISPATCH — ABC Transport, Driver [name] PICKUP: Port of Wilmington, DE [address] — [date] 0800–1600 · Booking/pickup #: [#] · Container #: [#] (port supplies chassis) · TWIC required · $32 gate fee · pull the loaded reefer container DELIVERY: [Receiver], Everett MA [address] — by 0500 [date], drop & hook · Reference/PO: [#] · live receiver contact: [#] RETURN: bring the EMPTY container back to Port of Wilmington — get the return receipt TRACK: GPS link required the entire trip. Send PU/DO in & out times + signed BOL/POD. Questions → [you] [cell] anytime.

⑧ Set up tracking

You send the MacroPoint / tracking link; the driver accepts and starts sharing location. (If a carrier won't track, that's a red flag — and a tracking fee applies.)

⑨ Pickup day

  • Confirm the driver is en route with an ETA to the port.
  • Driver checks in at the port gate (TWIC), pays the gate fee, and pulls the loaded reefer container on the supplied chassis.
  • You capture the container #, the time in / time out, and the BOL — and confirm the reefer is running at the correct setpoint.
  • Update the TMS to "Picked Up" and notify the customer.

⑩ In transit — track & trace

  • Monitor location + ETA on the tracking link; for reefer, confirm the temperature is holding setpoint.
  • Do a pre-delivery ETA check (e.g., ~2 hours out) so the driver makes the early-morning window.
  • Push proactive updates to the customer; escalate immediately if the truck is running late or the reefer alarms.

⑪ Delivery

  • Driver arrives in Everett by the window, drops the loaded container and hooks the empty (drop & hook).
  • Capture the delivery time in/out and the signed POD/BOL; note any OS&D or rejection on the spot (photos, receiver notes).
  • Update the TMS to "Delivered" and notify the customer.

⑫ Return the empty container

The driver returns the empty container to the Port of Wilmington and you capture the empty-return receipt. This is required — it's how you confirm the equipment is back, and it protects against per-diem / lost-container charges from the steamship line.

⑬ Documents + fees

You collect the full document set: signed POD, empty-return receipt, and any accessorial paperwork (lumper receipt, scale ticket). You apply any carrier fees that were triggered (e.g., a no-tracking fee or a late-POD fee per the rate con).

⑭ Invoice the customer

You invoice the customer the agreed rate plus any legitimate accessorials, with the POD attached.

⑮ Pay the carrier (or factor)

ABC submits their invoice + signed POD. You pay ABC — or their factoring company if they have an NOA on file — per the agreed terms (standard net terms, or the quick-pay option for a fee). BASE keeps the spread between the customer rate and the carrier rate (minus any quick-pay/factoring cost).

⑯ Close the load

With POD collected, customer invoiced, and carrier paid, you mark the load closed in the TMS. Done — start to finish.

The mindset: once you book it, you own it cradle-to-grave — booked, dispatched, tracked, delivered, documented, invoiced, paid, and closed. A load isn't "done" when the truck rolls; it's done when it's closed out and clean.
