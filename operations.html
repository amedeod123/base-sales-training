1What We Are

A licensed freight broker that controls real port contracts — and dispatches owner-operators to run them.

BASE Carriers sits in the middle of three parties. We hold the customer relationships (the people who own the freight) and the port credentials on one side, and a network of owner-operator trucking companies on the other. We connect the load to the truck and keep the spread.

The one-sentence pitch: "We pull containers out of the Port of Wilmington DE on direct contracts with Dole and Chiquita — we carry the insurance, the chassis pool agreements, and all the port credentials, so a one-truck operator can run port freight without spending $15K and months to get set up."

Why this is hard to copy

BASE has spent months and money building things a random broker can't replicate overnight:

  • Direct Dole & Chiquita contracts at the Port of Wilmington — not on any load board.
  • $4–5K/month of insurance binders carried to hold those contracts.
  • Chassis pool relationships, steamship-line (SSL) accounts, a BMC-84 broker bond and a $50K customs bond, plus an SCAC code and port admin.
  • A physical yard at 56 J Caldwell Lane, New Castle DE — minutes from the port.

2How We Make Money

Margin = what the customer pays us − what we pay the carrier − quick-pay cost.

Every load has two prices. The customer rate (revenue in) and the carrier rate (cost out). The job of the sales side is to raise revenue (land more/better customers, sell backhaul) while ops keeps the carrier cost down through the rate-bidding campaigns.

3A Load, Step by Step

The Everett MA round trip — a single overnight turn. Driver is home the next evening.
Day X · 8 AM–5 PMPickup at the port. Carrier arrives at Port of Wilmington with a tractor and a TWIC card, pays the $32 gate fee, the port supplies the chassis, and they hook a loaded 40' container of bananas (40,000 lbs).
overnightThe line haul north. ~330 miles up I-95 to Everett MA. They must leave DE by ~9 PM to make the morning window.
Day X+1 · by 5 AMDrop and hook. At the customer (e.g. a Boston-area customer), the driver drops the loaded box and hooks an empty — no waiting around to be unloaded. (Some lanes are live unload instead — see §5.)
Day X+1 · PMEmpty back to the port. The empty container returns to Port of Wilmington the same afternoon. Driver is home that evening.
Why carriers love it: single overnight turn, home the next night, steady weekly volume, drop-and-hook (no live-unload waiting), and Wilmington is an easier port than NY/NJ — it doesn't require UIIA registration.
The empty leg is the opportunity. That ~330-mile deadhead back south is 100% wasted today. Filling it with a paying southbound load is the Backhaul Program (§7) — pure profit because the truck is already making the trip.

6The Customers

Family-owned banana ripeners and importers across the Northeast — a small, named universe (not a numbers game).

Our customers buy full truckloads of Dole/Chiquita bananas out of the Port of Wilmington and ripen them in-house. They're multi-generational, relationship-driven, often voice-first and old-school. There are only a few dozen realistic targets — so the sales game is depth, not volume.

TAM (Total Addressable Market): the whole pie of customers we could ever serve. Ours is tiny and we know every name. The specific customer list (names, locations, contacts) is confidential — it's in the internal playbook. Ask Amedeo on day one.

9Trucking Lingo — Learn These Cold

If you can't speak the language, carriers and shippers tune you out in the first 10 seconds.

The core operational terms

Drayage
Short-haul trucking of an ocean container to/from a port. Our whole business is port drayage out of Wilmington.
Power Only
The carrier brings only the tractor (the power unit) — BASE/the port supplies the chassis and the container. Most of our lanes are power-only.
Tractor vs. Truck
A tractor is the semi cab that pulls a chassis/trailer. A "straight truck" or box truck can't pull a container — which is why we filter carriers for real tractors.
Chassis
The wheeled steel frame a container sits on so a tractor can pull it. The port supplies it from a chassis pool.
Container / Box
The 40' ocean container. High cube = 9'6" tall; standard = 8'6". Intermodal = it moves by ship, rail, and truck.
Drop & Hook
Drop the loaded box, hook an empty, leave. No waiting. Carriers love it.
Live Unload
Driver waits at the dock while freight is unloaded. Slow — can trigger detention.
Deadhead
Driving empty with no load (no revenue). "Ir vacío." Our southbound empty leg = deadhead we want to fill.
Backhaul
A load that fills the return/empty leg. "El regreso."
Line haul
The main long-distance leg of a trip (the rate for the haul itself, before accessorials).
Lane
A recurring origin→destination route (e.g. "the Everett lane").
TWIC
Transportation Worker Identification Credential — the federal port-access card. The one thing the driver must hold personally. 4–8 weeks to get.
Gate fee
What the port charges to enter ($32 at Wilmington, paid by the carrier).
BOL (Bill of Lading)
The legal document listing the freight — proof of what's on the truck. "El bill."
Rate Con (Rate Confirmation)
The signed sheet confirming the agreed rate & load details before a carrier rolls. "La confirmación."
Detention
Penalty pay when a driver is held past free time at a dock (usually after 2 hrs).
Demurrage
Port/terminal charge when a container sits past its free days before pickup.
Per Diem
Daily charge for keeping equipment (chassis/container) too long.
Lumper
A third-party paid to load/unload freight at a dock.
Accessorials
Extra charges on top of line haul — detention, gate fee, lumper, etc.
SSL (Steamship Line)
The ocean carrier that owns the containers (Maersk, MSC, etc.). We hold accounts with them.
UIIA
Intermodal interchange agreement required at most ports — Wilmington does NOT require it, which is a selling point.
BMC-84
The $75K surety bond every licensed freight broker must carry. We have it.
SCAC
Standard Carrier Alpha Code — the unique ID used in port/shipping systems.
MC# / DOT#
A carrier's federal authority (MC) and safety (DOT) numbers from FMCSA. How we look anyone up. Ours: MC-1299577 / DOT-3707179.
FMCSA
The federal agency that registers carriers. Its public census is where our carrier prospect lists come from.
Quick Pay / Factoring
Paying a carrier fast (1-day or 3–5 day) for a small fee instead of standard 30-day terms. Huge for cash-tight owner-operators. "Pago rápido."
Track & Trace
Monitoring a load's GPS location in transit (MacroPoint).
OTR
Over-the-road — standard long-haul trucking (vs. local/port work).

Cultural rules that win loyalty:
  1. Relationship first — ask about family before business.
  2. WhatsApp & voice notes beat email and calls.
  3. Word is bond — a verbal yes is a commitment, even before the rate con.
  4. Quick pay wins — cash flow is tight; "pago rápido" is a real selling point.
  5. Never lowball insultingly — it can damage the relationship permanently.
  6. "Ahorita" can mean now, soon, or later today — pin down an exact time.

11How We Actually Operate — Field Rules

Hard-won rules from the nightly grind. A rep who knows these sounds like an insider, not a salesperson.

🛡️ Default to truth

When a load runs late, tell the customer the truth. We only ever "buy time" with a cover story in two narrow cases: the driver is completely AWOL/no-showing, or the load isn't covered yet and we need minutes to book someone. A driver simply running 2 hours late? Just say so with a realistic new ETA. Honesty protects the customer relationship — and the relationship is the whole asset.

🚚 Always send the truck — let the customer decide

The instinct on a late load is "maybe they won't take it, let's cancel." Wrong. Always attempt delivery, even late. The accept/reject call belongs to the customer, never to us. Canceling a load we could have delivered is how you lose an account.

✉️ Issues go in writing — and the reference number goes in the subject line

For delays, problems, and late notifications, email beats phone. Email creates a record, lets the customer's receiving team plan, and pulls their sales rep into the chain (the rep is what actually moves a problem to a fix). Put the load/pickup reference number right in the subject — e.g. "297508 — not working." Keep the body short.

📞 Keep the driver in the loop or he walks

While you work a problem (calling the port, emailing the customer), send the driver steady updates: "Working on it, hold position, port's been contacted." A silent dispatcher is how a driver assumes he's being taken advantage of — and leaves the lot, abandoning the load.

🍌 Two customers, two maintenance procedures — never mix them up

If equipment breaks, the very first question is "Chiquita or Dole?" — because routing is completely different and using the wrong one means the repair doesn't get reimbursed:

OwnerProcedure
ChiquitaCall TTS directly — Chiquita pays the vendor, no out-of-pocket.
DoleEmail Dole M&R first, get a work-order number, then call the mechanic. We pay out-of-pocket, then invoice Dole.

For a roadside breakdown with no listed vendor: find 2–3 mobile truck-repair shops near the driver and hand over phone numbers. Don't punt routine problems upstairs.

12The Daily Rhythm & the Core Challenge

How a day actually runs — and the one bottleneck that shapes everything, including your job.

A day in dispatch

Customer sends the daily pickup list. Dole (and Chiquita) email the day's loads each morning — e.g. "BASE CARRIERS DOLE LOAD PICKUP FOR [DAY]." Some are flagged LTR (Last Truck Remaining) = priority.
Ops assigns each load to a carrier and the drivers head to the port to check in.
The customer monitors check-ins. If a driver hasn't checked in, they email "THE BELOW HAVE NOT CHECKED IN" — and we respond immediately with status. Fast, honest check-in responses are how we keep Dole/Chiquita happy.
Loads run, deliver overnight, empties return. Track & trace the whole way; handle exceptions with the field rules in §11.

The core challenge: carrier reliability

The hardest part of this business isn't finding freight — it's having a deep, reliable bench of carriers. A thin bench leads to double-booking (booking two carriers for one load as a hedge), late-night scramble coverage, and owner burnout — and it caps how much we can grow. That's why BASE invests in two things you should understand:

Expanding the carrier rolodex

Structured outbound to FMCSA-registered carriers — the goal is 30+ new operable carriers per quarter beyond the load boards. A deeper bench = fewer scrambles = ability to take on more customer volume.

Quick pay & loyalty

Paying carriers fast ("pago rápido") and treating them with respect builds a loyal, dependable bench. "Te cuido, me cuidas" — I look out for you, you look out for me.

Why this matters to you (sales): when you land a new customer, ops has to be able to cover the loads reliably. Don't oversell capacity we can't deliver — a blown delivery on a brand-new account burns the relationship and our reputation with Dole/Chiquita. Sell growth that ops can actually run.
